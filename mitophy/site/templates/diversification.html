Diversification of mitochondrial genomes

The mitochondrial genome of the last eukaryotic common ancestor already was a much-reduced bacterial genome, and every eukaryotic lineage has continued to lose genes to the nucleus (or altogether) at its own pace and in its own style. The result spans from the ~100-gene, bacteria-like mtDNAs of jakobids to the 3-gene, 6 kb genomes of apicomplexans and dinoflagellates, from 6 kb to 11 Mb in size, and includes at least ten different genetic codes.

Themes

  • Gene loss is the rule, but rates differ enormously. Jakobids retain ~66 protein genes including a bacterial RNA polymerase ({{ cite('Lang1997') | safe }}; {{ cite('Burger2013') | safe }}); animals keep 13, apicomplexans 3; the direction is nearly always mtDNA → nucleus ({{ cite('Adams2003') | safe }}). Gene-rich mtDNAs are scattered across the tree (jakobids, malawimonads, Ancoracysta, cryptophytes, some CRuMs and stramenopiles), so LECA's mtDNA was itself gene-rich ({{ cite('Janouskovec2017') | safe }}; {{ cite('Roger2017') | safe }}).
  • Size and gene content are decoupled. Land-plant mtDNAs reach megabases with only ~40 genes ({{ cite('Sloan2012') | safe }}); animal mtDNAs are compact; fungal genomes are inflated by introns and ORFs ({{ cite('SmithKeeling2015') | safe }}).
  • Architecture varies wildly: linear and multichromosomal genomes, fragmented genes and rRNAs (Chlamydomonas, apicomplexans, dinoflagellates, diplonemids), RNA editing (kinetoplastids, plants, Physarum), and repeated changes of the genetic code (UGA = Trp in table 4 mtDNAs, reassignments in animals, ciliates, green algae).
  • Complete loss of the mitochondrial genome occurred many times in anaerobes (mitosomes, hydrogenosomes) and, in Monocercomonoides, of the organelle itself ({{ cite('Karnkowska2016') | safe }}).
